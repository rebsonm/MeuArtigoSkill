# Evidence, claims and provenance

The Skill separates **source discovery**, **source access**, **semantic support**, **analysis** and **human validation**. None of these follows automatically from the previous step.

## Three research claim types

| Marker | Meaning | Minimum discipline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[L] Literature** | Claim grounded in actually consulted literature | Real source and `Evidence_ID`, precise locator, assess semantic support |
| **[I] Inference** | Researcher's analytical interpretation | Evidence, reasoning/warrant, scope and limitations |
| **[P] Proposition** | Original theoretical or practical contribution | Nearby prior work, explicit contribution delta and bounded novelty search |

**Empirical findings** need actual data and executed analysis; their provenance is more than choosing one of the three tags. A valid DOI identifies a source, but does not prove its text was read or that it supports a sentence. Likewise, a similarity or keyword match is not semantic verification.

## Distinct identifiers

The project can retain existing `Record_ID`/`Evidence_ID`, `Search_ID`, `Claim_ID`, `TRACE_ID`, `DEC_ID`, `GATE_ID`, `CADA_ID`, `SNAP_ID`, `AI_Use_ID` and `EXPORT_ID` **only where they solve an actual traceability need**. Do not create extra registries to make the project look sophisticated.

## An auditable claim

1. Cite the actual source or produced analysis.
2. Register the precise excerpt, location or output when accessible.
3. State what the source **does and does not** establish.
4. Separate direct literature support from new interpretation.
5. For originality claims, describe the searches performed and the **bounds of coverage**; never claim universal precedence from a finite search.
6. Obtain real researcher review for consequential final claims.

## Common invalid shortcuts

- Treating "DOI verified" as "full text read" or "claim supported."
- Presenting an AI screening proposal as a second **human** reviewer.
- Calling a source inaccessible through paywall "methodologically excluded."
- Reporting search results or empirical metrics without observed execution.
- Calling a human approval authenticated simply because a text field says so.
- Sharing copyrighted research PDFs just because the Skill's own code uses an open-source license.
