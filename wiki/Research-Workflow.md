# Research workflow

The lifecycle is **method-adaptive**. A numbered bibliographic-review procedure should not be imposed on an unrelated empirical or conceptual study.

## A practical end-to-end map

| Stage | Core question | Evidence of advancement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Problem | What question and contribution are intended? | Original researcher's formulation and reviewed scope |
| 2. Design | Which method fits the question? | Researcher-endorsed design and appropriate protocol |
| 3. Positioning | What prior knowledge or context is needed? | Documented and bounded sources/search, where relevant |
| 4. Materials | Which source corpus, empirical data or artefact are genuinely available? | Material identifiers, access conditions and review decisions |
| 5. Analysis | What was actually analyzed, synthesized or evaluated? | Original analysis outputs and limitations |
| 6. Claims | Which conclusions can the evidence support? | Reviewed [L]/[I]/[P] provenance and contrary evidence |
| 7. Manuscript | Does the journal submission package meet verifiable rules? | Checked manuscript files, declarations and actual submission receipt if submitted |

The seven corresponding **human scientific gates** are explained in [[Scientific gates|Scientific-Gates]]. A stage may contain many C.A.D.A. tasks, but task completion is not gate approval.

## For bibliographic review projects

After defining a genuine review route and protocol, a typical sequence involves designing search strings, recording actual searches, importing records, validating metadata, deduplicating, screening with real researcher judgments, tracking lawful full-text access, appraising evidence, synthesizing and drafting. Every count should be traceable to records and snapshots. An AI-generated shortlist is **not** a human-approved corpus.

Scopus/Web of Science may be accessed only when actually available. Where direct access is absent, the Skill may prepare a **reproducible search strategy** and process authentic exported records; it must not report that a planned query was executed.

## For empirical and conceptual studies

Use the same governance standards **without pretending the project has a review corpus**. Gate 4 may refer to real data/materials or artefact construction, and Gate 5 to executed analysis or evaluation. Ethics, participant rights and consent are assessed according to the real design and jurisdiction; no Skill can automatically confer ethics approval.

## Manuscript preparation

If a target journal is named, verify the official author guidelines and actual template before applying journal-specific formatting. If no journal is selected, keep the manuscript journal-neutral. Preserve author anonymity where required, rights to share full text, and a truthful AI-use declaration.
