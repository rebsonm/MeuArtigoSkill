## Purpose of this reference

Use this file before naming the review method. The workflow can contain systematic procedures without being a systematic review. Method labels must follow the research purpose and eligibility/synthesis design, not the sophistication of the tooling.

## Intake classification

From the user's research problem, identify the intended knowledge product:

- explain or integrate a fragmented body of literature;
- build/refine a framework, model, typology, mechanism map, propositions, or conceptual vocabulary;
- estimate/aggregate effects;
- map the scope of a field;
- test hypotheses with original data;
- analyze cases/documents/interviews/experiments;
- measure bibliometric structures;
- answer another clearly defined review question.

Do not force a method label before this is clear.

For justification, use the conditional [methodological foundations](methodological-foundations.md). Torraco (2005), Whittemore & Knafl (2005), Snyder (2019), and Elsbach & van Knippenberg (2020) inform integrative review design; Rethlefsen et al. (2021) informs transparent reporting of searches, **not** automatic recategorization as systematic. Alvesson & Sandberg (2011) may inform question problematization, while Bispo (2023) helps position the type of contribution. Empirical and design science sources apply when those methods are truly chosen.

## Integrative review

Prefer an **integrative literature review** when the goal is to integrate heterogeneous conceptual and/or empirical literature to produce a richer conceptual understanding, new framework, model, taxonomy, propositions, research agenda, or theoretical synthesis.

An integrative review may use rigorous and reproducible procedures such as:

- multiple indexed databases;
- frozen search strings;
- inclusion/exclusion criteria;
- documented screening;
- deduplication;
- full-text retrieval;
- evidence matrices;
- audit trails;
- backward/forward snowballing;
- purposive or theoretical deepening where explicitly justified.

These procedures make the review more rigorous; they do not by themselves make it systematic. **Keep critical appraisal/data evaluation as an explicit step**, distinct from CORE/SUPPORT prioritization and from DOI validity. The appropriateness of appraisal depends on each source's method, epistemic role and limitations (Whittemore & Knafl, 2005).

For literature-intensive conceptual articles, a defensible description is often:

"integrative literature review conducted through a structured and traceable bibliographic search and qualitative evidence synthesis."

If a purposive/theoretical full-text subset is used, state it plainly. Never imply that every pre-full-text candidate received full eligibility assessment when that did not happen.

## Systematic review guardrail

Call a review **systematic** only when its protocol and execution support that claim. At minimum, expect:

- a narrowly specified review question;
- explicit eligibility criteria;
- comprehensive and reproducible searching appropriate to the domain;
- transparent deduplication;
- systematic screening of all potentially eligible records;
- full-text eligibility assessment for the relevant candidate set;
- documented reasons for full-text exclusion;
- appropriate critical appraisal/risk-of-bias procedures where the design requires them;
- synthesis of the eligible evidence set;
- reporting consistent with the relevant systematic-review standard.

Do not convert a project into a systematic review merely because it uses PRISMA-like flow reporting, Scopus/WoS, or automated screening assistance.

PRISMA is primarily a reporting guideline for systematic reviews. Elements such as a transparent identification/screening flow can inspire reporting in other designs, but do not change the method label.

## Empirical article guardrail

For an empirical article, this skill can build:

- novelty audit;
- theoretical grounding;
- literature review;
- hypotheses/propositions only when logically derived and labeled;
- journal dialogue;
- source provenance;
- reporting scaffolds.

It must not invent:

- participants or observations;
- sample size;
- instruments;
- ethics approval;
- variables;
- data collection;
- statistical tests;
- interview coding;
- results;
- robustness checks;
- causal identification.

Require the actual empirical design and actual data/analysis before drafting empirical findings.

## Other review families

Do not substitute an integrative review for a requested or clearly appropriate:

- scoping review;
- meta-analysis;
- bibliometric review;
- realist review;
- umbrella review;
- rapid review;
- narrative review;
- qualitative evidence synthesis;
- domain-specific evidence-review method.

When one of these is indicated, retrieve the relevant methodological guidance before freezing the protocol.

## Reporting language

Prefer accurate procedural labels:

- structured bibliographic search;
- bibliographic database search;
- replication in a complementary bibliographic database;
- cross-database deduplication;
- incremental screening;
- title-and-abstract screening;
- full-text retrieval;
- evidence extraction;
- qualitative/integrative synthesis;
- purposive/theoretical sampling, when actually used;
- backward/forward snowballing.

Avoid inflated or incorrect labels such as:

- "meta-analysis" for combining databases;
- "triangulation" merely for Scopus × WoS overlap;
- "validated framework" when only conceptually synthesized;
- "systematic review" when full systematic eligibility procedures were not completed.

## Decision rules

1. Name the method from purpose + execution, not from tool sophistication.
2. Preserve a method decision in the protocol and update it only with justification.
3. If execution deviates from the intended method, fix the execution or relabel the method.
4. Never hide purposive sampling, partial full-text coverage, or tool-access limitations.
5. A transparent integrative review is methodologically stronger than an inaccurately labeled systematic review.
