# Research writing — ABCD, methodological fit, and AI evidence

This is original public **editorial guidance [I]/[P]**, not the text of a third-party teaching handout, a validated scientific method, or evidence [L].

## ABCD: four linked argument moves for an introduction

**A — Area and stakes.** Situate the phenomenon and its scientific conversation. Explain specifically who cares, why the problem matters, and what the most relevant sources substantiate. Do not start with a generic proclamation of importance.

**B — Boundary and tension.** Compare the nearest existing explanations. State what is established, where uncertainty or disagreement remains, and why closing that tension would change what is understood. An assertion that "few studies exist" is insufficient.

**C — Conceptual lens.** Identify the theory, concepts or mechanisms that make the problem answerable. State assumptions, plausible alternatives and limits. Distinguish sourced claims [L] from interpretation [I].

**D — Direction and contribution.** Write the precise question, matching research objective, feasible plan and a bounded *expected* contribution [P]. The contribution may be theoretical, practical, methodological or didactic. Never present proposed procedures or expected results as completed findings.

**Test:** A, B, C and D must refer to the same phenomenon and unit of analysis. They are *moves*, not mandatory four paragraphs. This ABCD formulation is an original operational synthesis and is **not** attributed as a verbatim model to any one cited author.

## Vertical coherence across the manuscript

| Link | Verify | Red flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Context -> problem | Specific consequential issue | Unsupported "gap" |
| Problem -> question -> objective | Same scope, unit and intended inference | Objective asks a new question |
| Question -> theory | Constructs and mechanisms defined | Decorative theoretical label |
| Theory -> data/design | Evidence can discriminate claims | Causal claim from only descriptive evidence |
| Data -> analysis | Sampling, measurement and comparison fit | Technique chosen before the question |
| Analysis -> contribution | Claims bounded by findings | Planned result treated as observed |

Label **planned**, **executed** and **verified** steps explicitly. Hypotheses are not a universal requirement: different research traditions have different analytic commitments.

## AI-assisted research and prompt traceability

For each consequential AI-supported research task, record (1) authorized data and source scope; (2) model/tool and relevant prompt/retrieval settings; (3) which original sources/pages were actually available; (4) alternative interpretations and negative cases; (5) human verification of DOI, passage and inference; and (6) [L]/[I]/[P] classification for accepted claims. An eloquent response, a filename or a private PDF is not proof or public redistribution permission.

Technical books on NLP, prompt engineering and software workflows are **prospecting leads** until the exact edition, bibliographic identity and rights have been checked. Do not reproduce protected books or course handouts in this repository.
